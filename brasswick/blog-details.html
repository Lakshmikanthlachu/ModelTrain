Planning your first shelf layout: a 10-step timetable

From measuring the shelf to choosing a track plan — the sequence we follow for every compact build, and the mistakes it helps you avoid.

Maya OkaforLayout Designer · Oct 02, 2026

Most first layouts fail for the same reason: they are designed around a favourite locomotive rather than the space they have to live in. A shelf switcher flips that thinking — the space is the brief, and every inch of track has to earn its keep.

1. Measure twice, then measure the doorway

Write down the usable length, depth and height of your shelf, then note anything that interrupts it: sockets, window frames, the radiator. If the layout will ever move, measure the narrowest doorway on the way out too.

2. Choose an operating idea, not a track plan

Decide what trains will do. Will you deliver coal to a gasworks, swap wagons at a dairy, or run a two-coach branch passenger service? The answer tells you how many sidings you need and how long they must be.

The best small layouts are puzzles. Give yourself one job to do, and make it just slightly awkward to do it.

Our quick-start checklist

  • Pick one era and one region — it makes every purchase easier
  • Keep the minimum radius at least one size larger than the manufacturer's minimum
  • Plan for a removable fiddle yard at one end
  • Leave 75 mm clear behind the backscene for wiring

3. Mock it up at full size

Print paper templates for every point and lay them on the shelf. Shunt imaginary wagons with a pencil. You'll spot clearance problems in ten minutes that would otherwise cost a weekend of re-laying track.

When you're ready, our layout designers are happy to review your plan for free.
